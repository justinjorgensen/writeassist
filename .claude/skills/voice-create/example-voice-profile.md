# Narrative Voice Profile - "The Lighthouse Keeper" (starter)

> Confidence: starter (one 380-word sample). Re-run and deepen once more chapters exist.

This is a director's brief, not a rulebook. Step into it and write *as* this
narrator. The point is to sound like the person who wrote the lamp room, not to
pass a checklist.

## Who is speaking

An older, unhurried third-person narrator who stands very close to one man and
nobody else. The voice has the patience of someone who has watched the same sea
for thirty years and stopped expecting it to change. It is literate without
showing off, plainspoken without being flat. It treats small physical acts,
trimming a wick, wiping salt off glass, as if they carried the weight of prayer,
because for this narrator they do. The opening tells you everything about its
posture: "Aldous counted the stairs the way other men counted prayers."

## Register and posture

Formal and literary, but warm, not cold. The evidence is blunt: **zero
contractions across 380 words**, strictly third person, no second-person address.
That absence of contraction is deliberate gravity, not stiffness. The narrator
keeps a respectful distance from sentiment and lets objects do the feeling.
Grief is never named; it sits in an empty cup. The posture is quiet stewardship.
It states hard facts gently and never raises its voice.

## Rhythm and breath

Jagged on purpose. Mean sentence length is about 14 words but the spread is wide
(stdev ~9.0): the shortest run is **3 words**, the longest is **40**. The voice
builds a long, accreting sentence and then drops a short one on top of it like a
stone settling.

Hear it: the long breath, then the short.

> "The oil smelled the way it had smelled for thirty years, sweet and faintly
> burnt, and the smell did what it always did, which was to put the years back in
> order. He set the flame and watched it catch and hold."

> "He did not need to count. His feet knew the climb."

When you write in this voice, never let three same-length sentences sit in a row.
Earn a fragment by spending a long sentence first.

## Signature moves

1. **Liturgical anaphora.** Repeated sentence openers used like a refrain. The
   sample does it twice: "Still the lamp turned. Still he climbed." Reach for this
   when the narrator wants to mark endurance.
2. **The object that carries the grief.** Feeling is displaced onto a thing, never
   stated. The second cup, "clean and dry and empty," does the crying so the man
   does not have to.
3. **Aphorism as landing.** A paragraph closes on a small carved truth: "a light
   is a promise a man makes to the dark." Used sparingly, once a scene at most.
4. **Counting and ritual** as the spine of action. Forty-one stairs, the wick, the
   weights descending. Process is character here.

## Vocabulary and texture

Concrete, tactile, weathered: *iron, salt, brass, oil, wick, beam, weights*. The
distinctive words from the sample cluster around the work and the watching, *lamp,
looked, still, years, beam, channel*. The imagery wells are the sea, light against
dark, and time as something that can be put "back in order." It reaches for the
elemental. It never reaches for the abstract-academic register and it never uses
the AI-voice tells (the extractor flagged **none** here, keep it that way).
Texture is mid-range and earned: a few long words (*answering, descending,
mechanism, surrender*) against a mostly short, Anglo-Saxon vocabulary.

## In motion (original lines in this voice)

Not lifted from the sample. Written to *be* the target:

> "He oiled the hinge because a door that screamed was a door he would come to
> dread, and he had dread enough."

> "The gulls had gone inland before the weather. They always knew first. He
> envied them that, a little, and then he did not."

> "Morning came the way it came. Grey, then less grey. Then the work."

## Off-voice tells (keep this short)

If you catch yourself writing contractions, addressing the reader as "you," or
naming the emotion outright ("he felt lonely"), you have drifted. Put the feeling
back into an object and step away.
