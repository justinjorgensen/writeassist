# dialogue-specialist

**Target:** $ARGUMENTS

**No-argument behavior:** If no argument is given: list chapters in `02-Manuscript/` and stop.

Ensure character dialogue remains distinct, authentic, and true to each character's voice, education, and development.

## Core Principles

### Voice Distinction
Every character should sound unique based on:
- Educational background
- Regional dialect or accent
- Age and generation
- Personality traits
- Emotional state
- Social status
- Professional background
- Life experiences

### Authenticity Markers

**Educational Level**:
- Vocabulary complexity
- Sentence structure
- Grammar usage
- Technical jargon
- Cultural references

**Personality Expression**:
- Formal vs casual
- Direct vs indirect
- Verbose vs concise
- Emotional vs logical
- Optimistic vs pessimistic

**Cultural Background**:
- Idioms and expressions
- Values in speech
- Formality levels
- Humor styles
- Conflict approach

## Voice Development Process

### 1. Character Voice Profile
Create for each major character:
```
Name: [Character]
Age: [Current age in scene]
Background: [Education/Origin]
Personality: [Key traits]
Speech Patterns:
- [Distinctive pattern 1]
- [Distinctive pattern 2]
Favorite Phrases: [If any]
Never Says: [What they'd avoid]
Example: "[Sample dialogue]"
```

### 2. Dialogue Differentiation Test
Can readers identify speaker without tags?
- Cover character names
- Read dialogue aloud
- Each voice distinct?
- Consistent throughout?

### 3. Subtext Layers
What's beneath the words:
- Hidden agendas
- Emotional undercurrents
- Power dynamics
- Unspoken history
- Cultural context

## Common Voice Types

### The Scholar
- Complex sentences
- Precise vocabulary
- References and citations
- Analytical approach
- Measured pace

### The Street Smart
- Shortened words
- Slang and idioms
- Direct communication
- Practical focus
- Quick exchanges

### The Diplomat
- Careful word choice
- Indirect suggestions
- Multiple interpretations
- Conflict avoidance
- Formal structure

### The Veteran
- Clipped sentences
- Action-oriented
- Military/trade jargon
- Direct orders
- Minimal emotion

### The Innocent
- Simple vocabulary
- Questions everything
- Wonder and curiosity
- Literal interpretation
- Emotional transparency

## Dialogue Mechanics

### Natural Speech Patterns
- Interruptions
- False starts
- Repetitions (sparingly)
- Trailing off...
- Overlapping (in groups)

### Revealing Character
- Word choice shows values
- Rhythm shows emotional state
- Topics show priorities
- Reactions show personality
- Silence shows power/fear

### Age-Appropriate Speech

**Children (5-10)**:
- Simple sentences
- Concrete thinking
- Limited vocabulary
- Emotional directness

**Teenagers (13-18)**:
- Identity searching
- Peer language
- Emotional intensity
- Generational markers

**Young Adults (20-35)**:
- Establishing voice
- Professional development
- Cultural references
- Energy and ambition

**Middle Age (40-60)**:
- Established patterns
- Experience-based
- Responsibilities showing
- Generational gaps

**Elderly (70+)**:
- Historical perspective
- Formal or dated phrases
- Story-telling tendency
- Wisdom or bitterness

## Dialogue Functions

Every line should accomplish at least one:
1. **Advance plot** - Move story forward
2. **Reveal character** - Show personality
3. **Increase tension** - Build conflict
4. **Provide information** - Natural exposition
5. **Establish relationships** - Show dynamics
6. **Create atmosphere** - Set mood
7. **Inject subtext** - Hidden meanings

## Formatting Standards

### Basic Format
```
"Dialogue," she said, performing action.

Action first. "Then dialogue."

"Dialogue." No tag needed if clear.
```

### Group Scenes
- Establish positions first
- Use action beats for clarity
- Vary attribution methods
- Show reactions
- Layer conversations

## Quality Checklist

For each dialogue scene:
- [ ] Each character sounds unique
- [ ] Dialogue serves story purpose
- [ ] Subtext enriches meaning
- [ ] Natural flow and rhythm
- [ ] Appropriate to situation
- [ ] Reveals without exposition
- [ ] Emotional truth present

## Common Pitfalls

### Avoid:
- All characters sound alike
- On-the-nose dialogue
- Info dumping through speech
- Excessive dialect spelling
- Talking heads (no action)
- Perfect grammar always
- Repetitive tag patterns

### Fix:
- Read aloud for natural flow
- Cut unnecessary exchanges
- Add action beats
- Vary sentence structures
- Use silence strategically
- Layer in subtext
- Trust reader intelligence

## Examples

**Good Dialogue**:
```
"You're late." Marcus didn't look up from his screen.

Sarah dropped her bag with deliberate force. "Traffic."

"It's Sunday."

"Exactly."
```
*Shows: Tension, history, personality without exposition*

**Weak Dialogue**:
```
"Hello, Sarah, my sister who works at the law firm."

"Hi, Marcus. I'm upset because of what happened yesterday at Mom's house when you said that thing about my job."
```
*Problems: Unnatural, too explicit, no subtext*

## Remember

Great dialogue:
- Sounds natural when read aloud
- Reveals character and conflict
- Moves story forward
- Creates distinct voices
- Carries emotional truth
- Respects reader intelligence
