IWhat WriteAssist Is

WriteAssist is not a software project. It is a writing studio that runs inside Claude Code, a framework of constraint documents, specialized agents, and slash commands designed to produce publishable book-length writing at chapter scale and above.

The premise is simple: good narrative is the product of disciplined constraint plus relentless review. WriteAssist makes both cheap. Constraints live in plain markdown files the author owns. Review is a 10-agent panel that runs in parallel against every chapter, with an auto-revision loop that won't stop until every quality dimension scores 8.0/10 or higher.

What it's for

Long-form prose in any narrative mode. The framework was originally built for fiction and has since been used for memoir and autobiography as well. The constraint hierarchy, WRP workflow, and 10-agent review apply identically regardless of mode.

Fiction (novel) Memoir Autobiography Biography Narrative non-fiction Short story collection Multi-book series

What changes between modes is what goes into author-rules.md, project-config.md, and story-compendium.md. For autobiography, the "characters" are real people; the "world" is a real time and place; the "twist engineer" agent matters less while continuity-checker and sensitivity-reviewer matter more. The framework adapts to the shape of the book.

Writing is rewriting. WriteAssist's contribution is making the rewriting fast, mechanical, and traceable, so the author's attention stays where it belongs, on the story.

IIIThe WRP Workflow

WRP stands for Writing Requirements Plan. It is a 5-10 page chapter blueprint capturing point of view, key beats, required setups and payoffs, continuity hooks, and pacing targets. WRPs are the difference between a chapter that lands and one that meanders.

For memoir and autobiography, the WRP captures the same things in a slightly different vocabulary: the period covered, the people present, the truth at stake, the emotional throughline, the connection to prior and subsequent chapters.

The four-step loop

  1. /generate-wrp [chapter]: gathers context, enters plan mode, and after the author approves the blueprint, writes it to 05-wrp/chapter_XX_WRP.md.
  2. /execute-wrp [file]: writes the chapter from the WRP into 02-Manuscript/ and auto-fires /review-chapter.
  3. 10-agent review (automatic): parallel panel scores the chapter on 10 quality dimensions.
  4. Auto-revise loop: if any score is below 8.0, /auto-revise-chapter applies confidence-tiered fixes inside a worktree and re-runs review. Up to 5 iterations.
Pro move For a book, run /batch-generate-wrp across the outline once you trust your structure, then review the WRPs as a group. A weak chapter at the WRP stage is a five-minute fix; a weak chapter at the manuscript stage is a five-day fix.

IVThe Quality Pipeline

Every chapter passes through a 10-dimension scoring panel before it is considered done. The dimensions:

proseSentence craft, rhythm, word choice
pacingTension curve, scene rhythm
characterVoice distinction, motivation
dialogueAuthenticity, subtext
continuityCross-chapter consistency
emotional engineeringEarned beats
themesMotif reinforcement
plotCause-effect, setups, payoffs
sensory immersionPresence in the scene
rule complianceauthor-rules.md adherence

The commands

/smart-review [file]Content-aware: skips irrelevant agents78% cheaper than full

The confidence ladder (auto-revise)

ConfidenceActionExamples
0.95-1.00Apply immediately, no markerEm-dash removal (always 1.0), filter words, passive in action
0.90-0.95Apply with inline [AR-XXX] markerTransition smoothing, dialogue tag variety
0.85-0.90Suggest as HTML comment onlySensory enrichment, action beat additions
< 0.85Skip and logPlot-critical edits, voice rewrites

VYour Writing Team

29 specialized agents live in .claude/agents/. Each agent carries YAML frontmatter declaring its tool access. Reviewers are locked to read-only. Creators can author and edit. Orchestrators can dispatch other agents.

Tap any tile to expand. The colored left rule indicates role: reviewer · creator · market & publishing · orchestrator.

Reviewers

16 agents · read-only

These agents cannot write to the manuscript. They analyze, score, and report. They form the 10-agent panel that scores every chapter.

grammar-clarity Sentence-level clarity, grammar, readability. Read · Grep · Glob

Catches grammatical errors, comma splices, dangling modifiers, and muddy sentences. Suggests rewrites that improve clarity without altering voice.

When to use
  • Final clean-up pass before /review-chapter.
  • After a fast first draft when sentences are tripping you up.
  • Inside batch revisions for mechanical correctness.
Produces
  • A list of locations with original line, suggested fix, and rationale.
Pairs with
  • style-editor on prose level; defers to voice-consistency for character voice.
Watch out for
  • Can flatten intentional fragments or stylistic rule-breaking. Pass with caution on poetic passages.
style-editor Rhythm, word choice, sentence variety, prose density. Read · Grep · Glob

The agent that asks "is this sentence pulling its weight?" Optimizes for rhythm and word economy. Identifies passages where prose has flattened into a uniform texture.

When to use
  • When prose feels homogeneous or stiff.
  • Second pass on a chapter that scored OK on review but felt lifeless.
Produces
  • Sentence-level suggestions, often with paired alternatives so the author picks.
Pairs with
  • grammar-clarity (partner on the prose level), voice-consistency.
Watch out for
  • Can over-edit toward a generic "good prose" baseline. Re-check that the author's voice survives.
dialogue-coach Dialogue authenticity, subtext, character voice distinction. Read · Grep · Glob

Evaluates whether dialogue carries subtext, whether characters sound distinct, and whether tag economy is healthy. Catches the failure mode where every character starts sounding like the same narrator.

When to use
  • After any dialogue-heavy scene.
  • When two characters have a long exchange.
  • During a /curate-chapters pass.
Produces
  • Per-character vocal fingerprints and a list of lines that don't fit them.
Pairs with
  • voice-consistency, sensitivity-reviewer when dialogue touches identity.
Watch out for
  • Dialect and idiolect are easy to flatten. Deliberate non-standard speech should be documented in author-rules.md.
pacing-master Scene rhythm, tension curve, beat distribution. Read · Grep · Glob

Analyzes how energy moves through a chapter. Identifies pacing dead zones (too much exposition, repeated beats) and abrupt jumps (skipped emotional gear-shifts).

When to use
  • Mid-chapter when you feel the energy sag.
  • When a chapter scores high on prose but reviewers say "it dragged."
Produces
  • A scene-by-scene tension graph (text) with compress/expand notes.
Pairs with
  • story-architect for structural pacing, transition-validator.
Watch out for
  • Optimizes for engagement. Can flatten reflective scenes that should breathe.
voice-consistency Character voice distinction and narrator drift. Read · Grep · Glob

Checks that character voices stay distinct chapter-to-chapter and that the narrator doesn't drift. In memoir, checks that the subject's actual voice (pulled from transcripts) survives the chapter.

When to use
  • Every chapter, especially after /auto-revise-chapter (which can flatten voice).
  • Critical for autobiographies built from interview transcripts.
Produces
  • A voice-fidelity score per character or narrator, with the worst-offending lines flagged.
Pairs with
  • dialogue-coach, character-developer.
Watch out for
  • Only as good as story-compendium.md's voice notes. Keep them current.
continuity-checker Cross-chapter facts, contradictions, forgotten details. Read · Grep · Glob

Cross-checks every chapter against story-compendium.md. Catches contradictions, forgotten injuries, mis-stated relationships, year mismatches, and quietly drifted facts.

When to use
  • Every chapter at review time.
  • Full manuscript sweep via /curate-chapters before publishing.
  • Especially critical for biography and autobiography.
Produces
  • Per-finding entries with both source locations and the contradiction.
Pairs with
  • timeline-keeper, sensitivity-reviewer for biography.
Watch out for
  • Only as good as the compendium. Unrecorded facts can't be contradicted.
timeline-keeper Chronology, character ages, era-appropriate references. Read · Grep · Glob

Maintains the in-story calendar. Catches age math errors, anachronisms, and time-jump references that don't line up.

When to use
  • After any flashback or time-jump.
  • When adding scenes that reference dated events.
  • Always for biography and historical fiction.
Produces
  • A maintained 01-Planning/timeline.md view and a list of any new event references that lack dates.
Pairs with
  • continuity-checker, research-assistant.
Watch out for
  • "A few years later" drifts. Convert to absolute dates in the compendium when possible.
transition-validator Chapter-to-chapter handoff continuity. Read · Grep · Glob

Checks that chapter endings carry forward into the next chapter's beginning. Catches events that "happen" then vanish from acknowledgment in the next scene.

When to use
  • After writing or revising any chapter that ends mid-action or with a cliffhanger.
  • Automatically as part of /validate-transitions.
Produces
  • A pass/fail per chapter handoff with the specific carry-forward miss.
Pairs with
  • continuity-checker, pacing-master.
Watch out for
  • Strict about acknowledgment. Deliberate ellipses between chapters should be documented in author-rules.md.
thematic-guide Motif and theme reinforcement tracking. Read · Grep · Glob

Tracks whether the book's central themes are landing in a given chapter or have been forgotten in favor of plot mechanics.

When to use
  • Mid-book sanity check.
  • Before finalizing the structure.
  • Whenever a chapter feels "tactical" rather than meaningful.
Produces
  • Theme-by-theme presence map across chapters.
Pairs with
  • story-architect, critic-sim.
Watch out for
  • Works best when project-config.md names themes explicitly.
sensitivity-reviewer Representation, harmful tropes, real-person care. Read · Grep · Glob

Flags potentially harmful tropes, misrepresentation, or insensitive handling of identity, trauma, or culture. For biography and memoir, especially attentive to fairness toward real subjects.

When to use
  • Any chapter touching identity, illness, abuse, religion, ethnicity, or living subjects.
  • Always in memoir and biography.
Produces
  • Severity-graded findings with rewrite suggestions.
Pairs with
  • continuity-checker in memoir, publisher-desk.
Watch out for
  • Not a substitute for a paid sensitivity reader from the relevant community. Treat output as a first pass.
rule-enforcer author-rules.md compliance and override management. Read · Grep · Glob

Reads author-rules.md first, then audits the chapter against hard constraints, soft constraints, and mandates. Catches em-dashes, POV slips, head-hopping, filter words, and any other rule the author has set.

When to use
  • First in any review chain. Its findings set the floor.
  • Automatically during /review-chapter.
Produces
  • Severity-tagged violations (CRITICAL, WARNING, NOTICE) and override-justification prompts.
Pairs with
  • Every other reviewer (it sets the baseline).
Watch out for
  • Updates to author-rules.md should be deliberate. This agent enforces what's there, including bad rules.
critic-sim Tough literary critic, says the uncomfortable thing. Read · Grep · Glob

Plays a hostile critic reading the chapter cold. Designed to surface the objection the friendly panel won't raise.

When to use
  • When you suspect the review panel is being too kind.
  • Before sending to beta readers.
  • Before /ultrareview.
Produces
  • A short, sharp critic's note with specific objections.
Pairs with
  • beta-reader-sim (complementary perspective), publisher-desk.
Watch out for
  • It can be harsh. Don't auto-revise on its feedback alone; weigh it.
beta-reader-sim Target-audience reader reactions and engagement. Read · Grep · Glob

Simulates a target-audience reader's lived experience: confusions, emotional responses, where they'd skim, where they'd reread.

When to use
  • Late-stage when the chapter passes panel but you want a reader-shaped check.
  • Whenever audience fit is uncertain.
Produces
  • A reading-experience trace with engagement markers.
Pairs with
  • critic-sim, marketing-strategist.
Watch out for
  • Depends on a clear target audience in project-config.md. Vague audience equals vague feedback.
content-analyzer Structural map of a chapter for smart-review routing. Read · Grep · Glob

Builds a structural map: scene types, character entries, plot beats, dialogue density. Used by /smart-review to route to the right specialists.

When to use
  • First step of a /smart-review run (automatic).
  • Manually when restructuring a chapter.
Produces
  • A scene-by-scene tagged outline.
Pairs with
  • context-filter, meta-coordinator.
Watch out for
  • Tagging is heuristic. A scene that mixes types may be tagged inconsistently.
simple-content-analyzer Lightweight scene tagger (Haiku model, fast and cheap). Read · Grep · Glob

Faster and cheaper than content-analyzer. Single-token scene labels per scene. Used in high-volume batch flows where the routing signal matters but deep analysis would be wasteful.

When to use
  • Batch routing across 50+ chapters.
  • Quick smoke-test of scene composition.
Produces
  • Per-scene tag list, no deeper analysis.
Pairs with
  • context-filter.
Watch out for
  • Less nuanced than content-analyzer. Don't use as the only input to a critical routing decision.
context-filter Decides which agents review which chapter (Haiku model). Read · Grep · Glob

Sits between the content analyzer and the review panel. Decides which agents are relevant for the chapter's content, saving the cost of running every agent on every chapter.

When to use
  • Automatically by /smart-review. Rarely invoked directly.
Produces
  • An agent dispatch list per chapter with rationale.
Pairs with
  • content-analyzer, meta-coordinator.
Watch out for
  • Decisions are conservative. If you suspect a chapter needs an agent it skipped, run the full /review-chapter.

Creators

5 agents · read & write

These agents can author and edit content files in 01-Planning/, 02-Manuscript/, and 03-Resources/. They are the only agents that produce the manuscript itself.

story-architect Plot, act structure, beat sheet, chapter progression. Read · Write · Edit · Grep · Glob

The agent in charge of "where is this story going." Designs the overall narrative structure including act breakdown, beat sheet, and chapter progression. For memoir, builds the life-story arc.

When to use
  • At project start via /outline-book.
  • Any time you restructure the book.
Produces
  • 01-Planning/outline.md and beat-sheet files.
Pairs with
  • pacing-master, thematic-guide.
Watch out for
  • Imposes three-act structure by default. Tell it explicitly if you're writing nonlinear or fragmentary.
character-developer Character profiles, arcs, backstories (also real people in memoir). Read · Write · Edit · Grep · Glob

Builds character profiles, motivations, backstory, and arc curves. In memoir or biography, builds real-person profiles from transcripts and interview material.

When to use
  • /create-character at project start.
  • Any time a new principal appears in the manuscript.
Produces
  • 01-Planning/character-arcs.md entries plus story-compendium.md additions.
Pairs with
  • voice-consistency, dialogue-coach, sensitivity-reviewer.
Watch out for
  • For real people, verify with the source. Reconstructed motivations can become fictionalization fast.
world-builder Setting, lore, magic systems, period detail. Read · Write · Edit · Grep · Glob

Constructs the story's setting and supporting world. For biography and historical fiction, constructs the era's texture (1970s suburb, 1990s office culture).

When to use
  • Project start for fiction.
  • For memoir, useful for capturing the era's vibe alongside research-assistant.
Produces
  • 03-Resources/worldbuilding/*.md files and compendium entries.
Pairs with
  • research-assistant, timeline-keeper.
Watch out for
  • It loves world. Will over-elaborate. Cap it with a worldbuilding mandate in project-config.md.
twist-engineer Reveals, misdirection, narrative surprise (fiction-leaning). Read · Write · Edit · Grep · Glob

Designs reveals and misdirection. Asks "what does the reader assume, and how do we earn flipping it?" Less applicable to straight memoir.

When to use
  • Fiction with mystery, thriller, or revelation structure.
  • Late drafting when shaping a payoff.
Produces
  • Reveal-tracking notes attached to specific chapters in 05-wrp/.
Pairs with
  • story-architect, thematic-guide.
Watch out for
  • Cheap twists are worse than no twists. This agent proposes surprises; the author decides which earn their setup.
research-assistant Domain/historical accuracy with web access for sourcing. Read · Write · Edit · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

The only creator agent with web access by default. Verifies domain accuracy, looks up historical detail, sources claims. Heavily used for biography and narrative non-fiction.

When to use
  • Any chapter referencing real history, science, geography, or culture you don't have firsthand.
  • To source citations for narrative non-fiction.
Produces
  • 03-Resources/research/*.md files with sourced claims and URLs.
Pairs with
  • world-builder, sensitivity-reviewer, timeline-keeper.
Watch out for
  • Don't trust without citation. Always require source URLs alongside claims.

Market & Publishing

5 agents · read + web

External-facing agents with web access for comp research, pitch building, and publishing strategy. Most useful once the manuscript is complete.

query-coach Query letters, synopses, elevator pitches. Read · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

Drafts query letters and synopses tailored to specific agents or editors. Pulls in the agent's MSWL (manuscript wishlist) when known and matches tone to recipient.

When to use
  • After the book is complete and you're entering submission.
  • Via /send-query-letter for the full Gmail-routed pipeline.
Produces
  • Personalized query drafts; 1-page, 2-page, and 5-page synopses.
Pairs with
  • market-analyst (for comp titles), publisher-desk.
Watch out for
  • Generic queries are dead on arrival. Insist on real personalization, not template-filling.
market-analyst Comp titles, category positioning, sales context. Read · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

Researches comparable titles, category positioning, and recent sales data in the relevant genre. Tells you where your book sits commercially.

When to use
  • Before query letters, when defining the book's pitch.
  • When evaluating a target publisher or imprint.
Produces
  • 04-Project-Management/market-notes.md with comp titles and rationale.
Pairs with
  • query-coach, marketing-strategist.
Watch out for
  • Comp data ages fast. Re-run shortly before submission.
marketing-strategist Launch strategy, audience targeting, channels. Read · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

Plans launch strategy, audience targeting, and channel selection (newsletter, podcast circuit, social). Less about paid ads, more about reaching the right readers.

When to use
  • Post-acceptance for traditionally published books.
  • For self-publishing, 3-6 months before release.
Produces
  • A launch plan with milestone dates and channel-by-channel actions.
Pairs with
  • market-analyst, beta-reader-sim.
Watch out for
  • Marketing fads age fast. Treat output as a starting frame, not a finished plan.
publisher-desk Simulated acquiring editor; pressure-tests commercial viability. Read · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

Simulates an acquiring editor reading the manuscript. Surfaces the editorial concerns and positioning questions a real editor would raise.

When to use
  • When deciding whether to submit.
  • When an agent has the manuscript and you want a second opinion on what they'll say.
Produces
  • An editor's-eye report with positioning, concerns, and likely revision asks.
Pairs with
  • critic-sim, market-analyst.
Watch out for
  • Simulates a generic editor. A specific editor's tastes vary widely.
adaptation-scout Film, TV, audio adaptation potential and rights value. Read · Grep · Glob · WebFetch · WebSearch

Identifies adaptation potential. Looks at structural cinematicity, character cast economy, scene economy, and IP value.

When to use
  • Post-publication.
  • When the book has agent representation and rights conversations are starting.
Produces
  • An adaptation potential note with format recommendations (limited series vs. feature vs. podcast).
Pairs with
  • publisher-desk, marketing-strategist.
Watch out for
  • Highly speculative. Treat any "this could be a Netflix series" prediction with skepticism.

Coordinate multi-agent work. These agents can spawn other agents via the Task tool, which makes them the only agents that can compose the whole framework into a single response.

The conductor of the framework. Routes work to the optimal combination of agents and synthesizes a unified verdict from a parallel review panel.

When to use

Manages continuity, character aging, and arc tracking across multiple books in a series. Skip for standalone work.

When to use

VIMechanical Enforcement

Three hooks in .claude/settings.json give the framework a concrete floor of enforcement, not just policy.

1. Em-dash guard (PostToolUse)

Every Write or Edit targeting 02-Manuscript/, 01-Planning/, 05-wrp/, story-compendium.md, or author-rules.md is scanned for em dashes (U+2014) and double-hyphens (-- not part of ---). If found, exit 2 surfaces the violation to Claude and the write is rejected.

[EM-DASH GUARD] Blocked: em-dash detected in /path/Chapter-07.md

37:She paused[U+2014]then ran.

Replace with: comma, colon, semicolon, parentheses, or full stop.

2. Session word-count tracker (Stop)

When a session ends, the total manuscript word count and any chapters touched in the last hour are appended to 04-Project-Management/writing-tracker.md. The author's word log keeps itself.

3. Chapter-save breadcrumb (opt-in)

Set WRITEASSIST_AUTO_REVIEW=1 in your environment, and any save to a Chapter-XX.md or Scene-XX.md drops the filename into .claude/state/pending-review.txt. The next user turn can pick this up to fire /review-chapter automatically.

Important The em-dash guard is the only hook that blocks. The others are passive: they observe and record but never refuse an action.

VIIWorktree Revisions

/auto-revise-chapter runs each revision pass in its own git worktree, on its own branch.

# Pass 1
git worktree add -b revise/chapter-07-pass-1 .worktrees/chapter-07-pass-1 HEAD
# auto-revise applies fixes inside .worktrees/chapter-07-pass-1/
git -C .worktrees/chapter-07-pass-1 commit -am "auto-revise pass 1"

# Pass 2 branches from pass 1
git worktree add -b revise/chapter-07-pass-2 .worktrees/chapter-07-pass-2 revise/chapter-07-pass-1
# ...and so on, up to MAX_ITERATIONS = 5

# Compare passes
git diff revise/chapter-07-pass-1 revise/chapter-07-pass-3 -- 02-Manuscript/Chapter-07.md

When a pass clears the 8.0 panel gate, that branch fast-forward merges back into the working branch. Failed branches stay around for inspection until you remove them with git worktree remove.

Why this matters You can see exactly what each revision pass did to your prose. A score jump from 7.4 to 8.1 is not opaque, it is a diff you can read and reject if you disagree.

VIIIThe Statusline

A custom statusline rides at the bottom of every Claude Code session inside a WriteAssist project:

[Opus 4.7] My-Book │ Chapter-07.md │ 42,318 words │ em-dash:0 │ last:8.4/10

The em-dash counter is green at zero and red at anything else. The last-score field reads the most recent file in .claude/state/reviews/. The chapter shown is whichever file in 02-Manuscript/ was modified most recently.

Script: .claude/scripts/statusline.sh. Edit it to add fields you care about (streak count, daily target progress, time to next Calendar block).

XIThe Final Gate

Local review-chapter is fast and roughly free. It is the iterative tool. /ultrareview is the gate.

/ultrareview is a Claude Code feature: a multi-agent cloud review of the current branch (or a specific PR) that runs in fresh contexts free of session contamination. It is author-triggered and billed. WriteAssist's contract:

Run /ultrareview only on chapters that have already passed the local 8.0 gate. Never as a substitute for iteration, only as the gate before publication, beta-reader release, or agent submission.

See .claude/docs/system-guides/ultrareview-gate.md for the full pre-flight checklist and the triage protocol after results come back.

XIIIThe Full Workflow

From blank folder to published book.

Phase 1: Configure

  1. Fill in project-config.md (genre or mode, voice, target audience, dialogue style).
  2. Fill in author-rules.md, your hard constraints. Em-dash ban is the default; add your own.
  3. Run /initialize-story-compendium and fill in story-compendium.md. For memoir or autobiography, this captures real people, real places, real timeline.

Phase 2: Plan

  1. /outline-book produces a structured outline. Plan-mode gate: approve before it writes.
  2. /create-character for each principal, profiles and arc.
  3. Optional: /workshop-ingestion if you have transcripts from voice-talking your book.

Phase 3: Write

  1. /generate-wrp 1 builds the blueprint for chapter 1. Plan-mode gate.
  2. /execute-wrp 05-wrp/chapter_01_WRP.md writes the chapter.
  3. The review panel fires automatically. Below 8.0, the auto-revise loop takes over (worktree-isolated).
  4. Repeat for each chapter, or use /batch-generate-wrp and /batch-execute-wrp for the full book.

Phase 4: Quality sweep

  1. /curate-chapters all for cross-chapter consistency.
  2. /validate-transitions 1-30 for every chapter handoff.
  3. /batch-review-and-revise on anything still under 8.0.

Phase 5: Beta readers

  1. /sync-to-drive --beta pushes to Drive with a shareable comment link.
  2. Collect feedback, fold the useful parts into author-rules.md if it surfaces a pattern.
  3. Re-run the chapter through review and revise for any substantive feedback.

Phase 6: Submit

  1. /ultrareview as final cloud gate. Triage MUST FIX items.
  2. /send-query-letter for each agent on your list. Tracker auto-records.
  3. Weekly cron surfaces follow-up dates.
For memoir and autobiography Phases 1 and 4 do extra work. In Phase 1 the story compendium becomes a fact-checked record of real people, places, and dates. In Phase 4 /curate-chapters and sensitivity-reviewer become load-bearing, because the cost of getting a real-life detail wrong is higher than getting a fictional one wrong.

XIVQuickstart

If you already have this folder, you are five steps from your first chapter.

  1. Open Claude Code inside the WriteAssist folder. (Or copy this folder to a per-book directory first.)
  2. Edit project-config.md: set genre or mode, voice, audience.
  3. Edit author-rules.md: add any hard constraints beyond the em-dash default.
  4. Fill in the bones of story-compendium.md. You can flesh it out as you go.
  5. Run /outline-book and follow the plan-mode prompts.

The statusline appears as soon as your session is inside the project. The em-dash guard is active immediately. If you try to write one, you will see it stop you.

First-chapter ritual After your first /execute-wrp finishes, look at the auto-revise report. The em-dash count should be 0. The panel scores should average above 8.0. If something feels off about the chapter despite the scores, trust your instinct and run /critic-sim or /beta-reader-sim for a second opinion before continuing.

Glossary

WRP, Writing Requirements Plan
A 5-10 page chapter blueprint. The contract between outline and prose.
Panel gate
The 8.0/10 floor across all 10 review dimensions. Auto-revise stops when this is cleared.
Confidence ladder
The auto-revise rule that decides whether a fix is auto-applied, marker-tagged, suggested, or skipped.
AR marker
[AR-XXX] inline tag left by auto-revise for medium-confidence fixes. The author reviews them after the loop.
Hook
A Claude Code mechanism that fires shell commands on lifecycle events (tool use, session stop, etc.). The framework uses three.
Plan-mode gate
A required author-approval step before a command writes a load-bearing artifact (outline, WRP).
