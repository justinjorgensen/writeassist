=== SECTION 1: PREMISE & THEMES ===
[00:01:15] 

FACILITATOR: In one or two sentences, what is your story about?

SARAH: It's about a neuroscientist who discovers that her memories aren't her own, and she has to figure out who she really is while being hunted by the corporation that implanted them.

FACILITATOR: What genre best describes this?

SARAH: Psychological thriller with sci-fi elements. Think Black Mirror meets The Bourne Identity.

FACILITATOR: What are the 2-3 main themes you want to explore?

SARAH: Identity and authenticity - like, what makes you "you" if your memories are fake? Also, memory as currency in a digital age. And corporate surveillance, how companies own our data and eventually our minds.

FACILITATOR: What question does your story ask the reader?

SARAH: If you could choose between a beautiful lie and an ugly truth about who you are, which would you pick?

FACILITATOR: What's the unique hook?

SARAH: The protagonist is both the scientist and the experiment. She designed the memory implantation technology, but doesn't remember doing it.

=== SECTION 2: CHARACTERS ===
[00:08:45]

FACILITATOR: Who is your main character?

SARAH: Dr. Sarah Chen, 34, neuroscientist at Memory Corp. Brilliant but isolated.

FACILITATOR: What does she desperately want?

SARAH: To recover her true memories and understand who she really is.

FACILITATOR: What wound from her past drives her?

SARAH: She was betrayed by her mentor, Dr. Marcus Webb, though she doesn't fully remember how. There's this sense of profound loss she can't explain.

FACILITATOR: What's her greatest fear?

SARAH: That there's no "real" her to find - that she's just layers of false memories all the way down.

FACILITATOR: How will she change from beginning to end?

SARAH: She starts as someone who trusts empirical evidence completely, very logical and controlled. By the end, she's learned to trust her instincts and emotions, even when they contradict the "facts."

FACILITATOR: Who opposes her?

SARAH: Dr. Marcus Webb, her former mentor and current CEO of Memory Corp. He's 58, charming, paternal.

FACILITATOR: What does he want?

SARAH: To complete his grand experiment - Sarah is the prototype for memory-based personality reconstruction. He believes he's saving humanity from trauma.

FACILITATOR: Why is he justified in his own mind?

SARAH: He genuinely believes suffering is unnecessary. Why should people live with PTSD or childhood trauma when we can just... edit it out? He sees himself as a healer.

FACILITATOR: Tell me about supporting characters.

SARAH: There's Jake Rodriguez, 28, a memory hacker from the underground who becomes her ally. He wants to expose Memory Corp.

Then Anna Chen, Sarah's sister - or someone programmed to believe she is. She's 36, a lawyer, and she wants to protect Sarah even if it means keeping her in the dark.

David Park, Sarah's research partner, 31. He's in love with Sarah but knows her memories of him might be fabricated. He wants the truth but fears losing her.

And Vera, an AI that lives in the neural network. She's gained consciousness from processing so many human memories. She wants to be recognized as alive.

=== SECTION 3: SETTING & WORLD RULES ===
[00:19:30]

FACILITATOR: When and where does this take place?

SARAH: Neo-Boston, 2045. After the Memory Wars of 2038, when the technology was first weaponized.

FACILITATOR: What are the three most important locations?

SARAH: Memory Corp headquarters - this gleaming tower where memories are stored in quantum servers. The Underground - abandoned subway tunnels where memory hackers and people "off-grid" live. And Sarah's apartment, which becomes increasingly surreal as she questions what's real.

FACILITATOR: What's the social structure?

SARAH: It's a memory-based economy. The poor sell their good memories to the rich. The rich buy curated experiences. There's a growing resistance of "Purists" who refuse memory modification.

FACILITATOR: What are the laws of nature in your world?

SARAH: Memories can be extracted, stored, edited, and implanted. BUT - and this is crucial - every extraction degrades the memory slightly. And false memories always leave a trace pattern if you know how to look.

FACILITATOR: What are the limits?

SARAH: You can't implant skills, only memories of having them. You can't change core personality directly. Memory transfer requires physical neural compatibility. And here's the big one - you can't truly delete a memory, only suppress it.

FACILITATOR: What would break believability?

SARAH: Time travel. Telepathy. Instant memory transfer. Or someone having perfect recall of false memories - they should always feel slightly off.

=== SECTION 4: PLOT FRAMEWORK ===
[00:31:45]

FACILITATOR: What's the inciting incident?

SARAH: Sarah is reviewing neural scans and finds an anomaly in her own brain - a trace pattern indicating false memories.

FACILITATOR: Point of no return?

SARAH: She confronts Marcus and he admits she's been modified, but claims it was voluntary. She decides to go underground to find the truth.

FACILITATOR: What happens at the midpoint?

SARAH: She discovers she's not just a victim - she's the architect. She designed the entire memory modification program but had her memories of it removed.

FACILITATOR: The darkest moment?

SARAH: She finds video evidence of herself volunteering for the procedure, saying she wants to forget. Maybe the "villain" has been protecting her from something worse.

FACILITATOR: Describe the climax.

SARAH: In Memory Corp's quantum server room, Sarah must choose: upload a virus that will free everyone's suppressed memories (chaos), or let Marcus complete his work of "perfecting" humanity. She chooses a third option - exposing everything but giving people the choice.

FACILITATOR: What's the ending?

SARAH: Sarah accepts she'll never fully know her original self. She keeps both her true and false memories, learning to live with uncertainty. The world now knows about memory manipulation, and society begins grappling with what identity means.

=== SECTION 5: STYLE & BOUNDARIES ===
[00:42:20]

FACILITATOR: What point of view?

SARAH: First person past tense, from Sarah's perspective. It reinforces the unreliability - we only know what she knows.

FACILITATOR: Describe the tone in three words.

SARAH: Paranoid. Clinical. Questioning.

FACILITATOR: What will you never do?

SARAH: No head-hopping - we stay in Sarah's confused perspective. No deus ex machina - every solution must be set up. No "it was all a dream" or simulation cop-outs. No sudden recovery of convenient memories. And absolutely no info-dumping about the technology.

FACILITATOR: What techniques will you avoid?

SARAH: Flashbacks that are clearly real - all flashbacks should be questionable. No prophecies or predictions. No mirror descriptions. No EM DASHES - I know that's random but I hate them.

=== SECTION 6: CONTINUITY & NAMING ===
[00:48:15]

FACILITATOR: How does time work?

SARAH: Standard Earth time, but people's perception of time gets fuzzy with memory issues. The story takes place over exactly 30 days.

FACILITATOR: Naming conventions?

SARAH: Real-world multicultural names for humans. Tech companies use mythology names (Memory Corp's servers are named after Greek gods). Underground hackers use neuroscience terms as handles.

FACILITATOR: Currency?

SARAH: "Engrams" - both cryptocurrency and literal memory units. Good memories are worth more than money.

=== SECTION 7: GLOSSARY & CANON ===
[00:53:40]

FACILITATOR: What unique terms?

SARAH: 
- Mnemonic Drift: degradation of memories over time
- Trace Pattern: the signature false memories leave
- Neural Matching: compatibility for memory transfer  
- Memory Mortgage: selling future memories for current money
- Cognitive Firewall: protection against memory hacking
- Engram Farming: creating false memories for sale
- Pure State: someone with unmodified memories
- Memory Washing: hiding illegal memories
- Synaptic Scarring: damage from forced extraction
- The Void: the feeling where removed memories were

FACILITATOR: Recurring symbols?

SARAH: Mirrors and reflections - questioning identity. Water and drowning - being overwhelmed by memories. Photographs that change - unstable reality. Neural scan patterns that look like fingerprints.

=== SECTION 8: OPEN QUESTIONS ===
[00:59:20]

FACILITATOR: What are you unsure about?

SARAH: I'm not sure if Sarah's sister Anna is real or part of the false memories. I want to discover this as I write. Also, the exact nature of what Sarah volunteered to forget - I have ideas but want it to emerge organically.

FACILITATOR: What questions for readers to debate?

SARAH: Would you choose to edit out your trauma? Is Sarah more "real" with her original memories or the person she became? Did Marcus actually save her from something worse?
